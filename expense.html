Design Strategy

Employee Expense Reimbursement.

Designing for B2B
TEAM PROJECT Trishita Mazumdar · Heeya Kapadia
02 Context.

A growing organisation still runs expenses the way it did when it was small.

Employees submit claims, managers approve them, finance pays them out, and auditors check the records. Every step works on its own. Put together, at scale, the process has become slow enough that the organisation wants it fixed.

Employees Managers Finance Auditors
03 The Problem.

The same claim causes a different kind of friction for everyone it passes through.

EMPLOYEES

Submission is confusing

Choosing a category, filling the right details and picking the correct receipt all take longer than they should.

MANAGERS

Claims arrive incomplete

Missing receipts, wrong category, date or amount, no business purpose, no pre-approval, then a round of follow-ups.

FINANCE

Verification is manual

Considerable effort goes into checking documents that should have been correct before they ever arrived.

EVERYONE

No visibility of status

Employees cannot see where a claim has reached, so they chase managers and finance for an update.

04 Executive Intent.

Make submission and approval simple enough that errors, delays and follow-ups stop being part of the process.

Everything in the design is judged against that one sentence.

DELIBERATELY OUT OF SCOPE Payroll Budgeting Procurement

Adjacent, tempting, and all three would have pulled the system away from the claim itself.

06 Tasks with priorities.

Three core tasks, and every failure lands on the employee waiting for money.

ACTORCORE TASKFREQUENCYIMPORTANCEFAILURE IMPACT
Employee Record expenses, attach receipts, and submit accurate claims for reimbursement. High High High — delays or rejected reimbursements.
Manager Review claims for completeness and compliance, then approve, reject, or return them. Medium–High High High — delays employee reimbursement.
Finance team Validate approved claims, resolve exceptions, and process reimbursements and records. High High High — payment delays and compliance issues.
07 Research.

Four things the research told us, and what each one meant for the design.

WHAT WE FOUNDWHAT IT MEANS FOR THE DESIGN

01Employees make the same mistakes in the same places, expense category, expense details, required information, and receipt selection.

→ Confusion is highest during data entry and categorisation, so that is where the interface has to do the work.

02Managers see the same rejections repeatedly, missing receipt, wrong category, date or amount, no business purpose, no pre-approval, policy-limit violations.

→ Manager reviews are dominated by recurring completeness and compliance issues, not judgement calls.

03The review itself is a fixed sequence: review expense → verify purpose → check policy → approve, reject, or send back.

→ The review is essentially a structured checklist, but the tool does not support it as one.

04The organisation’s own goal is to reduce errors, delays, and follow-ups, not to add controls.

→ Prevention beats correction. Anything caught before submission never becomes a follow-up.

THE INSIGHT
Nobody is making hard decisions.
They are fixing avoidable mistakes.

Wrong category, missing receipt, no business purpose. The manager’s review is a checklist the tool never offered, and the employee’s confusion happens minutes earlier, at the form.

10 Constraints.

What the design had to work within before a single screen was drawn.

OPERATIONAL

The system must fit the existing employee → manager → finance approval workflow without adding unnecessary steps or follow-ups.

LEGAL

Expense claims must maintain accurate records and protect employee financial and personal data for compliance and auditing.

TECHNOLOGY

The system may need to integrate with existing finance, HR, and approval systems to support automated processing and status updates.

11 Experience Direction

Four principles, chosen so the experience prevents problems instead of reporting them.

Guided

Take the employee through each step rather than presenting an empty form.

Simple

Make completing a claim short enough that it gets done the same day.

Clear

Say exactly what is missing or which policy applies, at the moment it matters.

Transparent

Show approval progress and reimbursement status without anyone having to ask.

12 Strategic Choices.
WE PRIORITISED Easy expense submission Clear policy guidance Error prevention Approval visibility

So employees complete claims accurately, delays drop, and reimbursement becomes faster and more transparent for everyone involved.

WE DELIBERATELY DID NOT Payroll Budgeting Procurement Anything beyond the claim

The system stays focused on submission, approval, compliance and reimbursement. Extra features would have complicated the one experience that had to get simpler.
